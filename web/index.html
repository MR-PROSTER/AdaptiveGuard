Equation 1

Multi-Factor Composite Risk Model

Calculated continuously every 0.5 ms to anticipate execution crises before deadline violations occur:

$$R_{total}(t) = 0.30 R_{util}(t) + 0.30 R_{laxity}(t) + 0.25 R_{overrun}(t) + 0.15 R_{deadline}(t)$$
R_util(t) Normalized cumulative utilization pressure of active jobs currently queued for CPU processing.
R_laxity(t) Inverse execution laxity $L_i(t) = (d_i - t) - c_i^{rem}(t)$, penalizing near-zero slack margins.
R_overrun(t) Active ratio of HI tasks that have exceeded their nominal $C^{LO}$ execution budget.
R_deadline(t) Urgency factor scaling with the absolute proximity of the earliest pending deadline.
Equation 2

Utility-Density Knapsack Service Degradation

When entering degraded states, LO tasks are prioritized by their utility-density ratio rather than uniformly dropped:

$$\rho_i = \frac{U_i}{C_i^{LO}}$$
Rank 1: Telemetry (L1) $\rho_1 = \frac{100.0}{6.0} = 16.67$ — Protected at full fidelity to preserve primary mission value.
Rank 2: Logging_A (L2) $\rho_2 = \frac{10.0}{1.0} = 10.00$ — Throttled first under moderate risk ($R \ge 0.50$).
Rank 3: Logging_B (L3) $\rho_3 = \frac{10.0}{1.0} = 10.00$ — Throttled second under elevated risk.
Rank 4: Logging_C (L4) $\rho_4 = \frac{10.0}{1.0} = 10.00$ — Suspended under extreme risk ($R \ge 0.80$).
Mechanism 3

Dual-Threshold Hysteresis Dampening

Eliminates mode chattering by enforcing asymmetrical transition boundaries:

$$T_{enter}^{WARN} = 0.50, \quad T_{enter}^{HI} = 0.80, \quad T_{exit}^{REC} = 0.40$$
Hysteresis Buffer Once in HI or WARNING mode, the system does not rapidly bounce back upon small load drops; it requires $R(t) \le 0.40$ sustained over time.
Gradual Multi-Step Recovery Restores degraded LO task budgets in controlled increments ($+25\%$ per recovery period) to prevent secondary overload spikes.
